## Premise

Monitor ~30 SaaS pricing pages (Stripe, Linear, Vercel, Notion, etc.) and
publish a monthly "Pricing Page Teardown" report. Sell to founders and PMs
who want competitive intel. $49–$299/yr or $19/mo.

## How coding automates it

- Scrape pricing pages weekly, diff against the snapshot, flag changes.
- LLM summarizes the change ("Linear added a free tier", "Vercel restructured
  usage-based").
- Static site auto-publishes the digest; paid subscribers get the full
  changelog and raw data.

## Evidence / Numbers

- "SaaS Pricing" newsletters (ProfitWell, OpenView) have been a thing for a
  decade. Most earn sub-$1k/mo from this kind of digest.
- The buyer pool is tiny — maybe 5k people in the world actively care.

## Cold-start plan

1. Pick a vertical slice (dev tooling, AI infra, CRM, etc.) — ~10 companies.
2. Scrape weekly, tweet every change you see.
4. After 6 months of consistent output, ask for paid subs.
5. Don't over-invest in tooling until you have paying readers.

## Risks & gotchas

- **The information is mostly free** — pricing pages are public. The premium
  is in synthesis and time savings, which is hard to defend.
- **Cold start** is brutal: nobody pays for a digest nobody has heard of.
- **Saturation**: half a dozen "SaaS pricing intelligence" companies already
  exist (PricingSaaS, RevVee, etc.).

## Verdict

Back-burner. Only worth doing as a side project that feeds your real
micro-SaaS business. Standalone it tops out at $500–$2k/mo and the moat is
zero.
