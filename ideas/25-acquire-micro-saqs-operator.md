## Premise

Buy a small, established micro-SaaS for $5k–$50k, optimize operations
(support, marketing, pricing), and either run it for the $300–$2k/mo
distributable cash flow or flip it for 2–3× revenue.

Marketplaces: **microacquire.com**, acquire.com, Flippa, SideProjectors.

## How coding automates it

- Once you own the SaaS, the only "automation" is doing the same
  work the original founder did, but faster (better docs, more
  marketing, fixing bugs).
- Most acquisitions are small enough that one operator can run them.
- Sourcing listings is the actual hard part — see Risks.

## Evidence / Numbers

- microacquire.com (HN 35567822, multiple posters): a marketplace
  itself for buying/selling micro-SaaS.
- HN thread 25434753 (33mail): acquired by a product studio in 2022
  from an independent developer.
- "I sold my micro-SaaS for $X" threads on IndieHackers, HN: typical
  sale = 24–36× monthly profit.
- "I bought a micro-SaaS for $X" threads: typical yield = 12–24 mo
  to recoup.

## Cold-start plan

1. Browse microacquire.com for products under $20k with a real
   revenue history (not projected).
2. Pick one whose stack you understand.
3. Verify revenue with seller (Stripe dashboard screenshots, GA traffic).
4. Negotiate. Escrow via the marketplace.
5. Optimize for 3–6 months.
6. Hold, sell, or repeat.

## Risks & gotchas

- **Sourcing is harder than it looks**. Good listings get snapped up
  within days; the average listing is bad.
- **LLM-disruption risk** (see `22-llm-replacing-micro-saas.md`): many
  of these are static-utility SaaS that customers can replace with
  LLMs. Buy with eyes open.
- **Capital risk**: $5k–$50k tied up in one bet. Diversify.
- **Asymmetric information**: the seller knows more than you do.
  Always check churn, refund rates, and the customer cohort.

## Verdict

Back-burner. Real business but sourcing + capital + LLM-disruption risk
make this harder than "buy a SaaS for $10k" sounds. Only viable if you
have (a) capital, (b) sourcing pipeline, and (c) operational chops.
